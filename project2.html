Interview Project

Learning From a Physical Therapy Professional

An informational interview connecting my Kinesiology studies, professional experience, and goal of becoming a physical therapist.

Interview Overview

Interviewee
A licensed physical therapist working in outpatient rehabilitation
Format
Informational conversation
Purpose
To better understand the education, daily responsibilities, and personal qualities needed in physical therapy.

What inspired you to become a physical therapist?

The therapist explained that helping people regain independence was the main motivation. Seeing patients return to activities that matter to them makes the demanding education and work meaningful.

What does a typical day look like?

A typical day includes evaluating movement, setting treatment goals, guiding exercises, documenting progress, and communicating with patients, families, and other healthcare professionals.

What skills matter most?

Communication, empathy, patience, critical thinking, and organization are essential. Technical knowledge matters, but patients also need to feel heard and encouraged.

What challenges should a student expect?

The therapist described the intensity of science courses, clinical training, and balancing time commitments. Staying consistent, asking for help, and learning from feedback are important habits.

What advice would you give an aspiring PT?

Observe different clinical settings, build strong study habits, and remember that patient care is both scientific and personal. Curiosity and professionalism should continue after graduation.

How does collaboration affect patient care?

Physical therapists regularly coordinate with physicians, occupational therapists, assistants, and families. Clear teamwork helps create safe, realistic, and patient-centered plans.

Project Reflection

How This Interview Shaped My Next Steps

This interview made my career goal feel more specific and realistic. I already knew that physical therapy requires a strong understanding of movement and rehabilitation, but I learned how much communication and trust shape every treatment session. The therapist's description of listening to a patient's concerns before choosing an exercise reminded me that healthcare is not only about knowledge; it is also about respecting each person's experience.

I also connected the interview to my current roles as a student, sales manager, and mother. My work has helped me practice listening, explaining ideas clearly, and staying organized when several responsibilities need attention. Those skills will support me in a clinical setting, but the interview also showed me areas I need to keep developing. I need to strengthen my science foundation, seek observation hours in more than one setting, and become more confident with professional healthcare vocabulary.

Most importantly, I left the conversation with a clearer picture of the commitment behind this career. The path will require discipline, clinical experience, and continued learning, yet the opportunity to help people recover their mobility is meaningful to me. I will use this insight to guide my course choices and continue taking practical steps toward a Doctor of Physical Therapy program.
