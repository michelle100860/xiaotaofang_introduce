Discussion Board & Assignments

Learning Through Conversation and Practice

A selection of eight course artifacts that document my participation, developing ideas, and progress as a learner.

01Career Exploration

What Can You Do With a Kinesiology Degree?

Areas of interest, career readiness, and next steps toward becoming a physical activity professional.

I am most interested in rehabilitation, exercise science, and physical therapy because they combine movement, health, and meaningful relationships with people. My Kinesiology studies give me a foundation in human movement, but I am still preparing for a professional role. I need stronger knowledge in anatomy and physiology, observation hours in clinical settings, and experience communicating with patients. My next steps are to continue my science coursework, seek volunteer opportunities in rehabilitation, and build the study habits needed for a Doctor of Physical Therapy program.

02Youth Sport

What Has Youth Sports Become and Who Is to Blame?

Parental involvement, coaching, positive experiences, and athlete burnout.

From an athlete's point of view, supportive parents can make sports feel safe and enjoyable, while pressure, criticism, and public arguments can cause embarrassment and anxiety. Coaches are not the only people responsible, but they set the tone for fairness, communication, and respect. As a coach, I would explain playing-time expectations before the season, focus feedback on effort and improvement, and create roles that help every player contribute. Parents should encourage, transport, and listen, but avoid trying to coach from the sidelines. Burnout happens when young athletes face too much training, too little rest, and the feeling that sport is a job instead of something they choose and enjoy.

03Motor Behavior Activity

Teaching Two Novel Motor Tasks

A teaching activity using a T-shirt and a weighted household object.

For this activity, I would teach a learner how to fold a T-shirt quickly and consistently, then teach a controlled movement sequence with a weighted water bottle. I would begin by demonstrating each task, give one instruction at a time, and let the learner practice without interruption. After each attempt, I would give specific feedback about the movement rather than simply saying “good job.” I would compare the learner's first and final attempts by noticing accuracy, smoothness, confidence, and the amount of help needed. This activity shows how demonstration, practice, feedback, and repetition support motor learning.

04Professional Practice

Job Duties for Health and Fitness Professionals

Internships, questions before choosing the major, and valuable pre-graduation experience.

An internship is important because it turns classroom concepts into professional practice. It lets Kinesiology students observe ethical standards, communicate with clients, and learn how a workplace operates. Before choosing this major, students should ask whether they enjoy helping people, are ready for science courses, understand the careers available, can complete required certifications or graduate school, and are willing to gain practical experience. Before graduation, I would like experience in a physical therapy clinic or rehabilitation setting. Observing patient evaluations and treatment sessions would help me understand the daily responsibilities of the profession I want to enter.

05Exercise Physiology

Improving Sports Performance or Fitness

A personal fitness goal, a measurement tool, and why it is useful.

I would like to improve my cardiovascular endurance so I can exercise longer with less fatigue. Before starting a training program, I would measure my resting heart rate and use a timed walk or step test to estimate how my body responds to exercise. I would repeat the same test after several weeks of regular training. These tools are useful because they are practical, inexpensive, and easy to compare over time. A lower resting heart rate or improved test performance would give me evidence that my heart and lungs are adapting to the training program.

06Sport Psychology

Supporting Savanna's Confidence at the Free-Throw Line

A consultation plan for performance anxiety and reduced confidence after missed free throws.

Savanna appears to be experiencing lowered self-efficacy and performance anxiety after a highly visible mistake. This reaction is common because athletes often replay a negative moment and begin to expect another failure. I would first listen to Savanna's account of the game and help her set a process goal, such as using the same breathing, cue word, and shooting routine for every free throw. We would use imagery to rehearse successful shots, practice positive and realistic self-talk, and gradually recreate game pressure during practice. I would also work with the coach so feedback focuses on the routine and effort instead of only the result. Bandura's self-efficacy theory supports building confidence through successful practice experiences, modeling, encouragement, and managing emotional arousal.

Source noted: Bandura, A. (1997). Self-efficacy: The exercise of control. W. H. Freeman.

07Professional Identity

Myths and Stereotypes of Kinesiology

Physical education stereotypes and the interdisciplinary nature of Kinesiology.

When people hear “physical education,” they may think only about sports, games, or a person who is naturally athletic. Common stereotypes are that the major is easy, that it is only for coaches, or that men should focus on strength while women should focus on appearance or children. These assumptions are limiting and unfair. Kinesiology includes anatomy, physiology, psychology, biomechanics, education, and health promotion. It prepares people for many careers that require scientific knowledge and communication skills. I think it is important to challenge these stereotypes because movement professionals serve people of different ages, abilities, and goals.

08Career Preparation

Required Certifications

Certifications, education, and future career choices in Kinesiology.

Required certifications depend on the occupation. Fitness professionals may need CPR/AED and a nationally recognized personal-training certification. Athletic trainers need an accredited degree, Board of Certification examination, and state requirements. Physical therapists need a Doctor of Physical Therapy degree, the National Physical Therapy Examination, and state licensure. Additional education may include a bachelor's degree, clinical internships, supervised practice, or graduate school. Based on what I know now, physical therapy is the career I am most likely to pursue because it combines my interest in movement with the opportunity to help patients regain function and independence.

Overall Discussion Reflection

What Eight Artifacts Show About My Growth

These discussions and assignments show that learning became more meaningful when I connected course ideas to my own life. Career exploration clarified the academic and clinical steps between my Kinesiology degree and physical therapy. The topics of coaching, youth sport, and motor behavior showed me that movement is not only physical; it is also shaped by confidence, relationships, and the environment.

The work also strengthened my professional judgment. I considered how internships, certifications, evidence-based practice, and clear communication prepare a student to work responsibly with others. In the sport psychology scenario, I learned that a strong professional does more than say “relax.” They provide practical tools, encouragement, and a structure for improving one step at a time.

Overall, these eight artifacts reflect my growth into a more intentional learner and future health professional. I learned to connect scientific knowledge with empathy, to question stereotypes, and to treat feedback as a resource. These are habits I will continue to use as I complete my Kinesiology education and prepare for a physical therapy career.
